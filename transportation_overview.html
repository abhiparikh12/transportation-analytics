Traffic Safety Program

The largest funding category — $18 million — targets every layer of the street safety system, from signals and signing to lighting and active transportation zones.

🚦
Traffic Signal Safety
Retro-reflective backplates — dramatically improve signal visibility at night and in adverse weather.
Pedestrian countdown timers — give pedestrians clear guidance on crossing time remaining.
Accessible push buttons — Accessible Pedestrian Signal (APS) devices with audio and tactile feedback for visually impaired users.
ADA curb ramp upgrades — bringing intersections into full federal accessibility compliance.
💡
Signage & Lighting
LED conversion — replacing induction streetlights citywide; lower energy cost, longer life, better uniformity.
Fluorescent sheeting on signs — 8,000 signs replaced. High-visibility retroreflective material performs 3× better in darkness and poor light.
Bike / Ped & School Zone Safety Countermeasures
High-Visibility Crosswalks
Ladder striping pattern — parallel edge lines with transverse rungs — visible to drivers from significantly greater distances than standard transverse lines.
RRFB Beacons
Rectangular Rapid Flashing Beacons at uncontrolled crossings — high-visibility amber flash pattern, federally recommended countermeasure.
Bike Boxes & Delineators
Protected green bike space at signal stop bars plus flexible post delineators to physically separate cyclists from vehicle lanes.
Bulbouts (Curb Extensions)
Sidewalk extensions that project into the parking lane — shortening the pedestrian crossing distance and calming turning vehicle speeds.
Blinking Stop Signs
LED-enhanced stop signs with amber border lights that flash to draw driver attention — improving compliance at low-volume intersections near schools.
Accessible Pedestrian Signal (APS)
Push-button devices with audio tones, speech messages, and vibrotactile feedback — giving visually impaired pedestrians safe crossing guidance.
⚠️
Advance Warning Signs
School zone and pedestrian advance warning signs alerting drivers well ahead of conflict points — paired with fluorescent yellow-green sheeting for maximum visibility.
Radar Feedback Signs
Dynamic speed feedback signs display the driver's actual speed alongside the posted limit — studies show reductions of 5–10 mph in school zones.
📋
Annual Safety Assessments
Annual school safety reviews, midblock crossing evaluations, speed limit reduction analysis, and conversion of static no-turn signs to dynamic LED versions.

New Operational Capabilities
Remote Access & Monitoring
Staff monitor and adjust signal timing from any location — dramatically reducing field visits and enabling faster incident response.
🚌
Transit Signal Priority
Bus routes receive green light extensions and red light truncations, improving on-time performance without degrading general traffic flow.
Adaptive Signal Control
Willow Pass Road adaptive system is live; Treat Boulevard in progress. Signal phases continuously adjust split, offset, and cycle length to real-time demand.
Improved Corridor Coordination
Time-space diagram–based green wave progression reduces stops and idling along major arterials — cutting travel time and vehicle emissions.

Bicycle & Pedestrian Projects

Three major capital corridors are transforming how Concordians walk and bike — connected to BART, downtown, and key community destinations.

🏙️
Downtown Bike Corridor
Salvio St (Broadway to Grant), Grant St (Salvio to Oak), Oak St (Grant to Galindo). Creates a connected network linking Todos Santos Park to the Concord BART Station.
🛤️
Galindo St. Multimodal Corridor
Galindo from Salvio to Clayton and Clayton from Galindo to Grant. A complete streets redesign serving cyclists, pedestrians, and transit riders.
🌿
Willow Pass Bike Lane Connection
Parkside Dr (Salvio to Willow Pass Rd) and Willow Pass Rd (Parkside to Landana Dr). Connects Willow Pass Community Park and Wren Avenue Elementary.
Implementation Funding Opportunities — Currently Pursuing
🏆
Monument Boulevard — Two-Way Cycle Track
Install a 1.6-mile Class I shared-use path on Monument Boulevard, from Mohr Lane in the west to Walters Way in the east.
📍
Pine Hollow Road Improvements
Bicycle and pedestrian safety improvements between Highlands Elementary and Pine Hollow Middle School. Feasibility study complete.
🔄
Farm Bureau Road Phase 3
Walnut Ave to Clayton Rd — Phase 1 & 2 improvements complete. Pursuing funding for Phase 3 with new Class II bike lanes and concrete curb & gutter.
🗺️
Cowell Road Corridor
Monument Blvd to Ygnacio Valley Rd. Evaluating design funding opportunities. Will serve as a key north-south active transportation link.

Transportation Management Center (TMC)
🎛️
Centralized Traffic Control Hub
The TMC will provide real-time visibility across the entire signal network — enabling operators to respond instantly to incidents, optimize signal timing, and coordinate with emergency services. Tentatively funded.
💡
Connected LED Streetlights
Smart streetlights serve as network nodes collecting speed, volume, and congestion data. Auto-alerts on outages, dynamic dimming to reduce energy costs, and longer operational lifetime than conventional fixtures.
